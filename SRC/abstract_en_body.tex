Medical image analysis quantifies organs, lesions, and tissue morphology to support disease diagnosis, treatment planning, and outcome assessment. Most deep learning methods assume that training data can be centralized, annotations are sufficiently complete, and data distributions remain stable. These assumptions often fail in clinical practice. Medical centers, scanners, and patient populations change over time, while new disease categories, anatomical structures, and imaging modalities continually enter the analysis workflow. Privacy protection, data governance, storage constraints, and annotation costs further restrict the information available for model updates: multi-center raw data may not be centralized, historical samples may not remain accessible, and current tasks may provide only sparse labels. The interaction between continual task evolution and limited training information requires a model to acquire new knowledge while retaining previously learned capabilities.

This thesis investigates three aspects of continual medical image analysis: scenario definition and evaluation for continual segmentation, replay-free federated continual classification, and task-level continual learning for weakly supervised segmentation and medical image registration. The main contributions are as follows.

(1) To address the lack of unified task scenarios, data construction, and evaluation criteria in continual medical image segmentation, as well as the inability of a single average-performance or forgetting measure to characterize model behavior, this thesis establishes a comprehensive benchmark. According to changes in the input distribution, label space, and segmentation target, the benchmark defines Domain-CL, Class-CL, and Organ-CL. It standardizes task sequences, model outputs, training budgets, and test settings, and evaluates representative methods in terms of final performance, historical knowledge retention, current-task modeling, forward generalization, and resource cost. The results show that lower forgetting does not necessarily indicate better new-task learning or forward generalization. The benefits of replay, regularization, and parameter expansion must also be interpreted together with historical-data access, task order, and resource cost. The benchmark therefore provides a clinically meaningful and reproducible basis for comparing continual medical image segmentation methods.

(2) To address cross-client forgetting when multi-center raw images cannot be centralized and historical samples cannot be replayed, while avoiding the excessive restriction of new-task learning caused by a single global protection space, this thesis proposes FedSubMerge, a gradient-subspace aggregation method for replay-free federated continual learning. Each client recursively constructs a low-rank principal gradient subspace from sample-wise gradients of the current task. The server aggregates historical protection directions across clients, and subsequent local training projects gradients onto the orthogonal complement of the protected subspace to reduce interference with knowledge accumulated across centers. An adaptive variant further selects related clients according to layer-wise subspace distances and constructs client-specific protection spaces. Experiments on pathology, abdominal computed tomography, gastrointestinal endoscopy, and real-world multi-center dermoscopy data show that the proposed methods improve overall classification performance and historical knowledge retention without reusing historical raw samples, and remain effective under different forms of data heterogeneity, client scales, and participation rates. Additional subspace communication occurs only at task boundaries, while global and adaptive aggregation emphasize historical stability and current-task plasticity, respectively.

(3) To address continual updates for medical image segmentation and registration when current supervision is incomplete, historical training data can be accessed only in a limited form, and models are susceptible to forgetting, this thesis proposes methods for continual segmentation with scribble supervision and sharpness-aware meta-continual registration. For segmentation, ZSDERpp uses partial cross-entropy on sparse scribbles, constrains unlabeled regions through global consistency and a spatial prior, and preserves historical knowledge through a capacity-limited image--scribble buffer, reference-feature retention, and weakly supervised replay. Domain-CL, Class-CL, and Organ-CL are handled through a shared output space, background-probability reorganization with previous-model constraints, and task-specific output heads, respectively. Studies across the three settings show that supervision enhancement mainly supplements sparse current-task labels, while limited replay and feature retention mainly mitigate historical forgetting; different forms of task evolution additionally require compatible output organization and semantic correction. For registration, SAMCL replays a capacity-limited set of historical image pairs, uses first-order meta-updates to coordinate optimization directions from current and historical tasks, and applies sharpness-aware training to both update paths. Results on four sequential registration tasks spanning different organs and modalities show that the method reduces early-task degradation caused by direct sequential training and improves performance on several seen and unseen tasks. Together, the two studies demonstrate that current-task learning, historical retention, and forward generalization should be evaluated in relation to the supervision form, historical-access condition, and type of task evolution.

In summary, this thesis addresses the central problem that continual task evolution and limited training information make it difficult for medical image models to balance new-knowledge acquisition, historical capability retention, and cross-scenario generalization. It develops a scenario and multidimensional evaluation framework for continual medical image segmentation, a gradient-subspace aggregation method for federated continual learning, and task-level continual learning methods for scribble-supervised segmentation and medical image registration. By clarifying task evolution, supervision form, historical access, and multi-center collaboration, this thesis provides methodological and experimental support for continual model updating, capability evaluation, and method design under limited training information.
