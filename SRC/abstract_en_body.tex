Medical image analysis models need to adapt to changing data sources, classes, organs, and imaging modalities while retaining previously acquired capabilities. Their updates are constrained by the cost of detailed annotation, restricted access to historical samples, and the difficulty of pooling data across centers. This thesis uses the task settings, data organization, and evaluation metrics of MedCL to connect a continual segmentation benchmark with weakly supervised continual segmentation, replay-free federated classification, and continual registration. The main contributions are as follows.

(1) MedCL is designed to evaluate continual medical image segmentation, classification, and registration through task sequences, stage models, fixed test sets, and scoring rules. Stage--task performance matrices support backward transfer rate (BWTR), restricted modeling ability (RMA), model parameter expansion (MPE), and data replay ratio (DRR). The segmentation benchmark covers six-center domain shifts, incremental cardiac structures, and cross-organ tasks, with extended forward transfer and whole-class Dice assessing setting-specific capabilities. In the six-center experiment, experience replay achieves a final average Dice of $0.750\pm0.021$, compared with $0.672\pm0.021$ for elastic weight consolidation. Their extended forward transfer scores are $0.256\pm0.150$ and $0.255\pm0.152$, respectively, showing that higher final performance need not imply better forward generalization.

(2) ZSDERpp combines supervision enhancement and feature consistency for weakly supervised continual segmentation. It reuses the six-center domain-incremental and three-task class-incremental settings of MedCL, replacing dense training labels with fixed simulated scribbles. Global consistency and spatial priors enhance supervision, while bounded image--scribble replay and reference-feature preservation support retention. On the six-center sequence, ZSDERpp achieves an average Dice of $0.7687$, compared with $0.5100$ for enhanced scribble-based sequential training, and improves BWTR from $-0.3240$ to $-0.1520$. Combined with MiB in the three-task class-incremental experiment, it achieves average Dice, BWTR, RMA, and whole-class Dice of $0.7793$, $-0.0034$, $0.9892$, and $0.7292$, respectively.

(3) FedSubMerge and its adaptive variant, FedSubMerge-AD, are proposed for replay-free federated continual classification. FedSubMerge aggregates client principal gradient subspaces and constrains shared-parameter updates through orthogonal-complement projection; the adaptive variant selects related clients separately for each layer. Evaluation uses sequential class batches, known test-time task identity, and task-specific classification heads, applying MedCL metrics to accuracy matrices. On PathMNIST with Dirichlet parameter $\alpha=0.3$, FedSubMerge-AD achieves final average accuracy of $93.42\%$, exceeding FOT by $7.22$ percentage points, with BWTR of $-0.2288$ compared with $-0.6127$ for FOT. On Hyper-Kvasir with $\alpha=0.3$, FedSubMerge achieves accuracy, BWTR, and RMA of $78.64\%$, $-0.1997$, and $0.9982$. Under full participation, additional stage-end subspace communication is approximately $3.0\%$--$3.5\%$ of the traffic for 20 regular model communication rounds.

(4) SAMCL combines bounded experience replay, first-order meta-continual learning, and sharpness-aware optimization for continual registration. It sequentially learns brain MR--MR, abdominal CT--CT, lung CT--CT, and abdominal MR--CT registration using limited historical image pairs, with stage-based and resource evaluation from MedCL. Compared with direct sequential training, SAMCL increases final brain Dice from $0.348$ to $0.697$ and improves backward change from $-0.395$ to $-0.037$. Its Dice-BWTR and Dice-RMA are $-0.0739$ and $1.0892$, compared with $-0.0871$ and $1.0156$ for meta-experience replay, which performs better on landmark-error metrics. SAMCL has zero MPE and DRR of $0.1806$.

Shared task settings, stage matrices, and multidimensional metrics connect these studies while preserving their task-specific outputs. The thesis examines new-task learning, historical retention, and resource use under sparse supervision, replay-free federated learning, and bounded image-pair replay, providing methods and evaluation foundations for continual medical image analysis.
