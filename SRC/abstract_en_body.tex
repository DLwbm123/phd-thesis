Medical image models must learn from new centers, anatomical labels, organs, and analysis tasks while retaining earlier capabilities. During an update, complete historical images may be unavailable, raw data from different hospitals may not be centralized, and segmentation training may provide only sparse scribble annotations. This thesis studies continual learning under these restrictions and distinguishes current-task learning, historical retention, and generalization to tasks not yet used for training.

The thesis is organized around a unified benchmark and platform interfaces, continual registration, weakly supervised continual segmentation, and federated continual classification.

(1) A benchmark basis for continual medical image learning is established, together with data, task, and evaluation interfaces for future platform integration. Medical image segmentation is used to instantiate domain-, class-, and organ-incremental scenarios. The benchmark contains a six-center prostate sequence, a three-stage cardiac-label sequence, and a four-stage cross-organ sequence with controlled patient splits, networks, training budgets, and evaluation points. In the six-center sequence, experience replay achieves a final average Dice of $0.750\pm0.021$, compared with $0.672\pm0.021$ for elastic weight consolidation. Their forward-generalization scores on centers not yet used for training are $0.256\pm0.150$ and $0.255\pm0.152$, respectively. Improved final performance therefore does not imply improved forward generalization. The benchmark separates final performance, retention, current-task modeling, forward generalization, and resource costs, and abstracts interfaces for data adaptation, task scheduling, stage-wise evaluation, and result tracking.

(2) Continual medical image registration is studied with limited replay of historical image pairs. SAMCL combines capacity-limited experience replay, first-order meta-continual learning, and sharpness-aware optimization to coordinate updates on current and historical tasks. In a four-task sequence covering brain MRI, abdominal CT--CT, lung CT, and abdominal MR--CT registration, final brain-registration Dice increases from $0.348$ with direct sequential training to $0.697$, while backward change improves from $-0.395$ to $-0.037$. For abdominal CT--CT, backward change improves from $-0.129$ to $-0.039$. SAMCL and meta-experience replay exhibit task-dependent trade-offs rather than uniform superiority. The stage--task matrix is extended to both Dice and target registration error, with replay capacity and performance on predefined unseen tasks reported separately.

(3) Weakly supervised continual medical image segmentation is studied with limited replay. Using the same data, task order, and test protocol as the benchmark, dense training masks are replaced with fixed scribbles. ZSDERpp combines global consistency, spatial priors, image--scribble replay, reference-feature constraints, and weakly supervised optimization on replayed samples. In the six-center prostate sequence, it achieves a final average Dice of $0.701$, an increase of $0.191$ over enhanced scribble-supervised sequential training without replay. Backward transfer rate improves from $-0.324$ to $-0.152$. Its forward-generalization score is not the highest, showing that stronger retention does not automatically improve applicability to future domains. These quantitative results concern fixed simulated scribbles and the evaluated domain-incremental configuration; they do not establish class- or organ-incremental performance.

(4) Federated continual medical image classification is studied without historical raw-sample replay. FedSubMerge merges important gradient directions from multiple clients at the server and constrains subsequent local updates. FedSubMerge-AD selects related clients using layer-wise subspace distances to retain more capacity for current-task learning. On PathMNIST with Dirichlet parameter $\alpha=0.3$, FedSubMerge-AD achieves a final average accuracy of $93.42\%$, exceeding the replay-free FOT baseline by $7.22$ percentage points. FedSubMerge achieves backward transfer of $-0.86\%$, compared with $-6.30\%$ for FOT. With full client participation, additional task-boundary subspace communication is approximately $3.0\%$--$3.5\%$ of regular model communication. Exchanging low-rank subspaces alone does not provide a formal privacy guarantee.

Together, these studies provide a traceable path from benchmark evaluation and platform interfaces, through task-level continual updates for registration and weakly supervised segmentation, to replay-free multi-center collaboration. They support continual medical image model development under explicitly defined information-access conditions.
