Medical image analysis models need to accommodate new centers, classes, organs, and imaging modalities while retaining previously acquired capabilities. Their updates are constrained by the cost of detailed annotation, restricted access to historical samples, and the difficulty of pooling data across centers. Differences in task sequences, output structures, and evaluation protocols further complicate the interpretation of current learning, retention, and forward generalization. This thesis develops the design of a continual learning evaluation platform and a segmentation benchmark, followed by studies of domain-incremental weakly supervised segmentation, class-incremental federated classification, and task-incremental registration. The three incremental settings are operationally defined by changes in input distributions, class sets, and medical analysis objectives, respectively. Output heads and test-time task identity are specified separately as evaluation conditions. The main contributions are as follows.

(1) MedCL is designed as an evaluation platform for continual medical image segmentation, classification, and registration. Its common workflow associates task order, stage models, test data, and evaluation results. Users train locally and submit models or predictions without providing training logs. Stage--task performance matrices organize continual evaluation, while a stage--client--test-task representation is designed for federated settings. The platform contribution comprises requirements, architecture, interfaces, interaction design, and a testing plan; the existing algorithm experiments were conducted independently of the platform. A continual segmentation benchmark covers six-center domain shifts, incremental cardiac classes, and cross-organ tasks, evaluating final performance, retention, current learning, forward generalization, and resource costs under controlled settings. In the six-center sequence, experience replay achieves a final average Dice of $0.750\pm0.021$, compared with $0.672\pm0.021$ for elastic weight consolidation. Their extended forward transfer scores are $0.256\pm0.150$ and $0.255\pm0.152$, respectively, showing that improved final performance does not imply a corresponding improvement in forward generalization.

(2) Domain-incremental weakly supervised segmentation is studied to reduce reliance on dense training annotations at newly arriving centers. Fixed simulated scribbles provide sparse supervision, while the prostate segmentation objective and dense test references remain shared across centers. Building on the static weakly supervised method ZScribbleSeg, ZSDERpp combines supervision enhancement, bounded image--scribble replay, and reference-feature consistency. Global consistency and spatial priors constrain unlabeled regions; feature preservation and weakly supervised replay optimization reduce interference with earlier domains. On the six-center sequence, ZSDERpp achieves a final average Dice of $0.701$, exceeding enhanced scribble-based sequential training at $0.510$ by $0.191$. Its backward transfer rate improves from $-0.324$ to $-0.152$. Its forward generalization is not the highest among the compared methods, indicating distinct trade-offs among supervision enhancement, retention, and performance on future domains. These quantitative conclusions are limited to fixed simulated scribbles and the domain-incremental setting.

(3) Replay-free federated continual classification is studied under expanding class sets, heterogeneous client distributions, and restricted access to historical raw samples. FedSubMerge aggregates clients' principal gradient subspaces at the server and projects subsequent updates onto their orthogonal complement to reduce first-order interference with cross-client historical knowledge. FedSubMerge-AD selects related clients separately for each layer to preserve more freedom for learning current classes. Evaluation uses known test-time task identity and task-specific classification heads. On PathMNIST with Dirichlet parameter $\alpha=0.3$, FedSubMerge-AD achieves a final average accuracy of $93.42\%$, improving on the replay-free baseline FOT by $7.22$ percentage points. FedSubMerge attains backward transfer of $-0.86\%$, compared with $-6.30\%$ for FOT. Under full participation, the additional subspace communication at a stage boundary is approximately $3.0\%$--$3.5\%$ of the traffic for 20 regular model communication rounds. Model and subspace summaries remain available, so replay-free operation does not mean the absence of historical information; subspace exchange does not provide a formal privacy guarantee.

(4) Task-incremental registration is studied across changing organs, modalities, and registration relationships. Registration predicts continuous spatial transformations, and these changes introduce distinct deformation-estimation objectives for a shared model. SAMCL combines bounded experience replay, first-order meta-continual learning, and sharpness-aware optimization to balance adaptation and retention with limited historical image pairs. In a sequence comprising brain MR--MR, abdominal CT--CT, lung CT--CT, and abdominal MR--CT registration, final brain Dice increases from $0.348$ for direct sequential training to $0.697$, while backward change improves from $-0.395$ to $-0.037$. Abdominal CT--CT backward change improves from $-0.129$ to $-0.039$. SAMCL and meta-experience replay have different strengths across tasks; the results support improved retention in the specified sequence rather than uniform superiority on all registration metrics.

Through common scenario descriptions and multidimensional evaluation, this thesis connects platform design, a segmentation benchmark, and three task-specific methods. The findings emphasize the need to specify task evolution, available training information, and evaluation conditions, and to assess current learning, retention, and applicable forward generalization separately. The work provides empirical foundations for continual medical image analysis under restricted information and defines the interfaces and evaluation requirements for subsequent platform implementation and validation.
