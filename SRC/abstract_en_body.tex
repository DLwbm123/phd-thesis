Medical image analysis can extract and analyze information about organs, lesions, and tissue structures from images, providing important support for disease diagnosis and treatment planning. As medical centers, disease categories, target organs, and imaging modalities vary over time, models need to continually learn new tasks while retaining their existing analytical capabilities. However, inconsistencies in task settings and evaluation procedures across existing studies make it difficult to comprehensively compare current-task learning, knowledge retention, and generalization under the same conditions. Meanwhile, limited training information in practical applications poses three challenges to model updating. First, pixel-level annotations are costly to obtain, and sparse labels provide insufficient guidance for learning complete target structures. Second, multicenter images are difficult to pool, and historical raw samples cannot be continuously accessed, making knowledge retention across clients more challenging. Third, changes in organs and modalities alter registration objectives and deformation distributions, making it difficult to balance new-task adaptation and old-task retention using only a small number of historical image pairs. These three constraints jointly hinder continual model updating and the preservation of previously acquired capabilities. To address these evaluation and methodological challenges, this thesis focuses on evolving medical image analysis tasks under limited training information, develops a medical imaging continual learning evaluation platform, and investigates methods for weakly supervised segmentation, replay-free federated classification, and registration with limited replay.

To address inconsistencies in task settings and evaluation procedures that hinder comprehensive comparisons of continual learning capabilities, this thesis presents MedCL, a medical imaging continual learning evaluation platform. For segmentation, classification, and registration, the platform establishes unified specifications for evaluation scope, continual learning settings, and evaluation metrics, and standardizes data splits, task order, and training conditions. While retaining task-specific output formats and the directions and units of the underlying task metrics, MedCL uses consistently defined continual learning metrics to assess knowledge retention, current-task learning, and generalization. It also analyzes resource requirements in terms of parameter growth and data replay costs, providing a common basis for subsequent method comparisons and experimental analyses.

To address inconsistent evaluation conditions for continual segmentation methods and the combined challenges of insufficient supervision and forgetting under sparse annotations, this thesis conducts continual segmentation benchmark evaluations using the platform’s standardized configurations and proposes ZSDERpp, a weakly supervised continual segmentation method. The benchmark covers cross-center domain shifts, incremental expansion of cardiac structure classes, and task changes across organs, comparing representative methods under consistent data splits, network architectures, and training settings. Experiments show that reducing forgetting does not necessarily improve new-task learning or generalization to future domains, and that method performance is also affected by parameter growth, replay buffer capacity, and task order. ZSDERpp uses global consistency and spatial priors to provide additional training constraints for unlabeled regions. It combines limited replay of historical image–scribble pairs with reference feature preservation to revisit past-task samples during optimization while constraining representation drift. For scenarios with incrementally expanding class sets, the method further accounts for background semantics so that old-class retention and new-class learning use compatible output representations. Finally, experiments are conducted in six-center domain-incremental and three-stage class-incremental settings, using the same case-level data splits, task order, and dense test annotations as the segmentation benchmark. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in improving segmentation performance and knowledge retention under fixed simulated scribble annotations.

To address federated continual classification when historical raw samples cannot be replayed and client distributions differ substantially, this thesis proposes FedSubMerge, a method based on cross-client fusion of principal gradient subspaces. Each client constructs a low-rank subspace from historical gradients, and the server fuses knowledge-preserving directions from different clients. Subsequent updates to shared parameters are projected onto the orthogonal complement of the resulting subspace to reduce interference with previously acquired knowledge during new-task learning. An adaptive fusion strategy further selects relevant clients on a layer-by-layer basis, reducing the constraints imposed by irrelevant historical directions on current learning. Finally, experiments are conducted in a multi-head classification setting in which classes arrive in batches and task identities are known at test time, covering distribution skew, label-quantity skew, and source-related feature skew. The results verify the effectiveness of both fusion strategies without raw-sample replay and show that their relative performance varies with client distributions.

To address the difficulty of balancing adaptation and retention in registration models as organs and modalities continually change, this thesis proposes SAMCL, a continual registration method that combines limited experience replay, first-order meta-continual learning, and sharpness-aware optimization. The method uses a small number of historical fixed–moving image pairs to provide training signals for previous tasks, employs meta-continual learning to coordinate update directions for current and historical samples, and incorporates loss variations within a neighborhood of the model parameters into optimization. Following the platform’s stage-wise evaluation procedure, structural overlap and landmark error are used to assess registration accuracy, knowledge retention, and current-task modeling capability. Finally, experiments are conducted on a task sequence comprising brain, abdominal, lung, and cross-modal abdominal registration, with additional analyses of replay buffer capacity, within-domain generalization, and cross-task generalization. The results show that the proposed method mitigates performance degradation on previous tasks during sequential updates across tasks and improves structural overlap on multiple registration tasks.
