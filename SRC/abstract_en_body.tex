Medical image models must learn from new centers, anatomical labels, and organs while retaining earlier capabilities. Updates may have access to only limited historical images, distributed hospital data, or sparse scribble annotations. This thesis studies continual learning under these restrictions, distinguishing historical retention, current-task learning, and generalization to untrained tasks.

The thesis is organized around a unified evaluation basis, replay-free multi-center collaboration, and task-level methods with limited replay.

(1) A scenario and evaluation framework is established for continual medical image segmentation. Domain-incremental learning changes centers or acquisition conditions while retaining the segmentation target; class-incremental learning progressively adds anatomical labels to a shared output space; organ-incremental learning introduces different organs and modalities as successive tasks. The benchmark includes a six-center prostate sequence, a three-stage cardiac-label sequence, and a four-stage cross-organ sequence, with controlled patient splits, networks, training epochs, and evaluation points. In the six-center sequence, experience replay achieves a final average Dice of $0.750\pm0.021$, compared with $0.672\pm0.021$ for elastic weight consolidation. Their forward-generalization scores on centers not yet used for training are $0.256\pm0.150$ and $0.255\pm0.152$, respectively. Improved final performance does not imply better forward generalization. Retention, current-task learning, generalization, parameter growth, and replay costs require separate evaluation.

(2) Federated continual classification is studied without historical raw-sample replay. FedSubMerge merges important gradient directions from different clients at the server and constrains subsequent local updates to reduce interference with cross-client historical knowledge. FedSubMerge-AD selects related clients using layer-wise subspace distances to retain more capacity for current-task learning. On PathMNIST with Dirichlet parameter $\alpha=0.3$, FedSubMerge-AD achieves a final average accuracy of $93.42\%$, exceeding the replay-free FOT baseline by $7.22$ percentage points. FedSubMerge achieves backward transfer of $-0.86\%$, compared with $-6.30\%$ for FOT. With full client participation, additional task-boundary subspace communication amounts to approximately $3.0\%$--$3.5\%$ of regular model communication. The client-training, server-merging, and stage-evaluation functions distinguish the existing implementation from future system integration. Subspace exchange alone does not provide a formal privacy guarantee.

(3) Continual segmentation and registration are studied with limited historical replay. Segmentation predicts pixel labels, whereas registration estimates spatial transformations; each therefore requires a task-specific learning objective. For scribble-supervised segmentation, ZSDERpp combines global consistency, spatial priors, image--scribble replay, and reference-feature constraints. In the six-center prostate sequence, it achieves a final average Dice of $0.701$, an increase of $0.191$ over enhanced scribble-supervised sequential training without replay. Backward transfer rate (BWTR) improves from $-0.324$ to $-0.152$. These results concern fixed simulated scribbles and the evaluated replay configuration, without establishing class- or organ-incremental performance. For registration, SAMCL combines limited image-pair replay, first-order meta-learning, and sharpness-aware optimization. In the four-task sequence, final brain-registration Dice increases from $0.348$ with direct sequential training to $0.697$, while backward transfer (BWT) improves from $-0.395$ to $-0.037$. SAMCL and meta-experience replay exhibit different task-dependent trade-offs, rather than uniform superiority of either method.

These studies provide verifiable evidence for continual medical image model updates through controlled evaluation, federated collaboration and engineering functions, and task-specific methods under defined information-access conditions.
