Medical image analysis quantifies organs, lesions, and tissue morphology to support disease diagnosis, treatment planning, and outcome assessment. Most deep learning methods assume that training data can be centralized, annotations are sufficiently complete, and data distributions remain stable. These assumptions often fail in clinical practice. Medical centers and scanners change over time, while new categories, anatomical targets, and imaging modalities continually enter the analysis workflow. Privacy, governance, storage, and annotation costs further restrict the information available for model updates: multi-center raw data may not be centralized, historical samples may not be replayed indefinitely, and current tasks may provide only sparse labels. Under these combined constraints, a model must acquire new knowledge without losing capabilities learned earlier.

This thesis studies four aspects of continual medical image analysis: scenario definition and evaluation for continual segmentation, replay-free federated continual classification, continual segmentation with scribble supervision, and continual image registration. The main contributions are as follows.

(1) A comprehensive benchmark is established for continual medical image segmentation. According to changes in the input distribution, label space, and segmentation target, the benchmark defines Domain-CL, Class-CL, and Organ-CL. It standardizes task sequences, model outputs, training budgets, and test settings, and evaluates representative methods in terms of final performance, historical knowledge retention, current-task modeling, forward transfer, and resource cost. The results show that lower forgetting does not necessarily indicate better current-task learning or forward transfer. Benefits from replay and parameter expansion must also be interpreted together with their information-access assumptions and resource costs.

(2) FedSubMerge is proposed for replay-free federated continual medical image classification. Each client recursively constructs a low-rank principal gradient subspace from sample-wise gradients of the current task. The server merges historical protection directions across clients, and subsequent local training projects gradients onto the orthogonal complement of the protected subspace to reduce interference with knowledge accumulated across clients. FedSubMerge-AD further selects related clients according to layer-wise subspace distances and constructs client-specific protection spaces. Experiments across medical imaging modalities, data-heterogeneity settings, client scales, and participation rates show that both methods improve overall performance and knowledge retention without replaying historical raw samples. Additional subspace communication occurs only at task boundaries.

(3) ScribbleCL is developed for continual segmentation with sparse scribble supervision, together with a method named ZSDERpp. For the current task, partial cross-entropy is complemented by global consistency and a spatial prior to constrain unlabeled regions. For historical tasks, a capacity-limited buffer of image--scribble pairs, reference features, and weakly supervised replay is used to preserve previous knowledge. The framework covers Domain-CL, Class-CL, and Organ-CL. Domain-CL uses a shared output space under sequential center shifts, Class-CL reorganizes background probabilities and constrains the updated model with the previous model when the label space expands, and Organ-CL uses task-specific heads for sequential changes in organs and modalities. Across the three settings, supervision enhancement primarily supplements sparse current-task labels, while replay and feature preservation address historical knowledge retention; setting-specific output organization and semantic correction remain necessary for different forms of task evolution. Continual segmentation with scribble supervision should therefore be evaluated in terms of historical retention, current-task modeling, and forward transfer in addition to final average performance.

(4) SAMCL, a sharpness-aware meta-continual learning method, is proposed for continual medical image registration. Based on a capacity-limited replay buffer of historical image pairs, the method uses first-order meta-updates to coordinate optimization directions from current and historical tasks and applies sharpness-aware training to both update paths. Results on four sequential registration tasks spanning different organs and modalities show that SAMCL reduces the degradation of early-task performance caused by direct sequential training and improves several task-specific and forward-generalization results. Its performance nevertheless remains dependent on task order, memory capacity, and the evaluated registration task.

These studies show that continual medical image analysis cannot be characterized by final average performance or forgetting alone. How tasks evolve, how much supervision is available for the current task, how historical evidence is retained, and how multi-center data participate in collaborative learning all affect the scope of a method's conclusions. By making these conditions explicit, this thesis provides corresponding scenarios, evaluation tools, and learning methods for continually updating medical image analysis models under limited training information.
