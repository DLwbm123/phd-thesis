Medical image analysis models need to adapt to changing data sources, classes, organs, and imaging modalities while retaining previously acquired capabilities. Their updates are constrained by the cost of detailed annotation, restricted access to historical samples, and the difficulty of pooling data across centers. This thesis presents the design of MedCL, a continual learning evaluation platform, establishes a continual segmentation benchmark, and studies cross-center weakly supervised segmentation, replay-free federated classification with expanding class sets, and continual registration across organs and modalities. The main contributions are as follows.

(1) MedCL is designed to evaluate continual medical image segmentation, classification, and registration through task sequences, stage models, fixed test sets, and scoring rules. Stage--task performance matrices record model performance, with a client-level extension designed for federated evaluation. The platform contribution includes requirements analysis, system architecture, evaluation interfaces, and a testing plan; the algorithm experiments were conducted independently of the platform. The segmentation benchmark covers six-center domain shifts, incremental cardiac structures, and cross-organ tasks, assessing final performance, retention, current-task learning, forward generalization, and resource costs separately. In the six-center experiment, experience replay achieves a final average Dice of $0.750\pm0.021$, compared with $0.672\pm0.021$ for elastic weight consolidation. Their extended forward transfer scores are $0.256\pm0.150$ and $0.255\pm0.152$, respectively, showing that higher final performance need not be accompanied by improved forward generalization.

(2) ZSDERpp combines supervision enhancement and feature consistency for weakly supervised continual segmentation. Building on the static method ZScribbleSeg, global consistency and spatial priors provide additional constraints beyond sparse scribbles, while bounded image--scribble replay and reference-feature preservation reduce forgetting during sequential updates across centers. Training uses fixed simulated scribbles; dense validation labels support model selection, and dense test labels support evaluation. On the six-center sequence, ZSDERpp achieves a final average Dice of $0.701$, compared with $0.510$ for enhanced scribble-based sequential training. Its backward transfer rate improves from $-0.324$ to $-0.152$, although its forward generalization is not the highest among the compared methods.

(3) FedSubMerge and its adaptive variant, FedSubMerge-AD, are proposed for replay-free federated continual classification. FedSubMerge aggregates client principal gradient subspaces at the server and constrains subsequent shared-parameter updates through orthogonal-complement projection. FedSubMerge-AD selects related clients separately for each layer to reduce constraints from unrelated historical directions. Evaluation uses sequential class batches, known test-time task identity, and task-specific classification heads. On PathMNIST with Dirichlet parameter $\alpha=0.3$, FedSubMerge-AD achieves a final average accuracy of $93.42\%$, exceeding the replay-free baseline FOT by $7.22$ percentage points. FedSubMerge achieves backward transfer of $-0.86$ percentage points, compared with $-6.30$ percentage points for FOT. Under full participation, additional stage-end subspace communication is approximately $3.0\%$--$3.5\%$ of the traffic for 20 regular model communication rounds. The methods retain model and subspace summaries without replaying historical raw samples; subspace exchange itself provides no formal privacy guarantee.

(4) SAMCL combines bounded experience replay, first-order meta-continual learning, and sharpness-aware optimization for continual registration. A limited set of historical image pairs participates in subsequent training to balance adaptation and retention as organs, modalities, and registration relationships change. The experiment sequentially learns brain MR--MR, abdominal CT--CT, lung CT--CT, and abdominal MR--CT registration. Compared with direct sequential training, SAMCL increases final brain Dice from $0.348$ to $0.697$ and improves backward change from $-0.395$ to $-0.037$. For abdominal CT--CT, backward change improves from $-0.129$ to $-0.039$. SAMCL and meta-experience replay have different strengths across tasks.

These studies show that continual medical image analysis should be evaluated through current-task learning, retention, and forward generalization in addition to final performance. By preserving task-specific data, models, and metrics, this thesis provides a platform design and experimental studies across three task types to support continual model updates under limited training information.
